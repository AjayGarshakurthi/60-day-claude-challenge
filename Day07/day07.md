## 🧠 When to Use Each Model

Use when speed matters more than depth.

- ✅ Quick concept explanations
- ✅ Grammar and spelling checks
- ✅ Simple code snippets
- ✅ Summarizing a paragraph
- ✅ Basic Q&A and definitions

---

### ⭐ Sonnet — Primary Model (Use This Most)
Use for the majority of daily student tasks.

- ✅ Essay drafts and outlines
- ✅ Research paper structure

- ✅ Blog posts and social media content
- ✅ Marketing plans and business case drafts

- ✅ High-stakes academic writing
- ✅ Market research with multi-factor synthesis

## ⚠️ Biggest Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Using Opus for every task** — It's slower and consumes more of your usage limits. Reserve it for genuinely complex work.

2. **Leaving effort on Max by default** — This burns through limits fast. Standard covers 80% of student needs.

3. **Writing vague prompts** — Avoid "help me write an essay." Always specify topic, length, audience, and tone.

4. **Not iterating** — Claude's first draft is a starting point. Ask it to refine, expand, or restructure for much better output.

5. **Using Haiku for deep research** — It lacks the reasoning depth needed for academic work. Don't sacrifice quality on important tasks.

## 💡 Pro Tips

- **Build a prompt library** — Save well-crafted prompts for repeated tasks (research outline, code review, marketing brief). Reuse them with the right model + effort combo.
- **Always iterate** — Use Claude's output as a first draft, then ask it to improve, expand, or restructure.
- **Be specific** — The more context you give (topic, audience, format, length), the better the output.
- **Use Claude Code** for coding-heavy sessions where you need multi-file context awareness.
